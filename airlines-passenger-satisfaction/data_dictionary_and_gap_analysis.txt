AIRLINE PASSENGER SATISFACTION
DATA DICTIONARY AND HYPOTHESIS GAP ANALYSIS
============================================================

Source file
-----------
File: data/raw/airline_passenger_satisfaction.csv
Rows: 129,880 passenger survey records
Columns: 24
Duplicate complete rows: 0
Missing values: 393 in Arrival Delay; none detected elsewhere

Important interpretation note
-----------------------------
The dataset does not include an official codebook. The descriptions below are
based on column names and observed values. The exact meaning of a rating of 0
is not documented and should be confirmed with the data provider before it is
treated as a genuine low score.

   customer.

7. Flight Distance
   Type: Integer, numeric
   Observed range: 31 to 4,983

   Type: Integer, numeric
   Observed range: 0 to 1,592

   Type: Numeric
   Observed range: 0 to 1,584

   Quality note: 393 values are missing (about 0.30% of all records). The unit

SUFFICIENCY FOR TESTING THE DEFINED HYPOTHESES
==============================================

Overall conclusion
------------------
The dataset is sufficient to test statistical associations for all three
hypotheses. Its large sample size and relevant variables support comparisons,
association tests, and multivariable models. However, it is observational and
does not support causal claims by itself.

Hypothesis 1: Higher cleanliness, seat comfort, and on-board service ratings
are associated with higher overall satisfaction.

Assessment: SUFFICIENT FOR ASSOCIATION TESTING

Available variables:
  - Cleanliness
  - Seat Comfort
  - On-board Service
  - Satisfaction

Possible analysis:
  - Compare satisfaction rates across rating levels.
  - Use a chi-square trend/association test or logistic regression.
  - Include passenger and travel variables as controls in regression.

Gaps and limitations:
  - The meaning of rating 0 is unknown. It could mean the lowest score, not
    applicable, or not used; this must be resolved or tested in sensitivity
    analysis.
  - Service ratings and overall satisfaction appear to come from the same
    survey, so common-response bias may strengthen observed associations.
  - No survey questions, scale definitions, or collection methodology are
    supplied.
  - The data can show association but cannot prove that improving a particular
    service will cause satisfaction to increase.

Hypothesis 2: Business-class passengers have a higher satisfaction rate than
economy and economy-plus passengers.

Assessment: SUFFICIENT FOR GROUP COMPARISON

Available variables:
  - Class
  - Satisfaction
  - Useful control variables: Type of Travel, Customer Type, Age, Gender,
    Flight Distance, delays, and service ratings

Possible analysis:
  - Calculate satisfaction rate for each class.
  - Use a chi-square test for differences among classes.
  - Use logistic regression to adjust for observable passenger and flight
    differences.

Gaps and limitations:
  - Class is not randomly assigned. Differences may be influenced by travel
    purpose, customer type, distance, price, loyalty benefits, or other factors.
  - Fare paid, ticket type, loyalty-program status, route, aircraft, airline,
    and upgrade status are unavailable, leaving important potential confounders.
  - Economy Plus has substantially fewer records (9,411) than Business (62,160)
    or Economy (58,309), although it is still large enough for testing.
  - Results can establish a difference between groups, not prove that cabin
    class itself caused that difference.

Hypothesis 3: Longer departure or arrival delays are associated with lower
overall satisfaction.

Assessment: SUFFICIENT FOR ASSOCIATION TESTING

Available variables:
  - Departure Delay
  - Arrival Delay
  - Satisfaction
  - Useful control variables: Class, Type of Travel, Customer Type, Flight
    Distance, Age, Gender, and service ratings

Possible analysis:
  - Compare satisfaction across delay-duration bands.
  - Model delay duration continuously with logistic regression, checking for a
    nonlinear relationship and the influence of extreme delays.
  - Analyze departure and arrival delays separately because they are related
    but represent different parts of the journey.

Gaps and limitations:
  - Arrival Delay has 393 missing values. The missingness pattern should be
    checked before records are excluded or values are imputed.
  - Delay units are not explicitly documented.
  - Extreme values reach more than 1,500 units and should be validated rather
    than automatically removed.
  - Scheduled flight duration, route, weather, cancellation/diversion status,
    reason for delay, and whether delays were communicated or compensated are
    unavailable. These factors could affect both delay and satisfaction.
  - The data can identify association but cannot prove that delays caused the
    reported satisfaction outcome.


RECOMMENDED ACTIONS BEFORE ANALYSIS
===================================

1. Obtain or locate the original dataset documentation to confirm measurement
   units, rating labels, and the meaning of zero ratings.
2. Check whether missing Arrival Delay values are concentrated in particular
   passenger groups, classes, or delay patterns.
3. Treat Satisfaction as a binary outcome and clearly state that "Neutral or
   Dissatisfied" is a combined category.
4. Report unadjusted group comparisons and adjusted models separately.
5. Use cautious language such as "associated with" rather than "caused by."
6. Perform sensitivity checks that compare results when zero ratings are kept,
   treated as missing, or analyzed as a separate response category.
